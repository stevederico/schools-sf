# SF School Age Cutoffs & Cohort Placement

Quick reference on how age cutoffs work across San Francisco preschools, TK, kindergarten, and private K-8 — and how to think about pushing a child up or holding them back.

---

## The Core Rule: September 1

Almost every school in SF uses **September 1** as the age cutoff. A child's age on **Sept 1** of the school year determines which class they're placed in.

- Born **Sept 2, 2023** → 2yo cohort for 2026-27 (they're still 2 on Sept 1, 2026)
- Born **Aug 31, 2023** → 3yo cohort for 2026-27 (they turn 3 just before Sept 1)

One day can shift a whole school year.

---

## By School Type

### SFUSD Public Preschool (Early Education / ELFA)
- **Free for SF residents ≤110% AMI** via ELFA
- Eligibility: "born between Sept 2, YYYY and Sept 1, YYYY+1" — same cutoff
- Fall 2026-27: kids born Sept 2, 2021 → Sept 1, 2022

### Transitional Kindergarten (TK) — SFUSD
- California has expanded TK to **universal** for all kids who turn 4 by Sept 1
- Public (free) through SFUSD
- Strict cutoff — no flexibility

### Kindergarten — SFUSD
- **Must turn 5 by Sept 1** to enter K
- Early entrance is technically allowed by California ed code but SFUSD rarely grants it
- Requires formal cognitive/developmental assessment showing significant above-age performance

### Private Preschools
- **Usually Sept 1 cutoff**, but more flexible
- Most will do a tour/observation before deciding placement
- Smaller schools (family daycares, small preschools) more flexible
- Larger formal programs (Cypress, Presidio Knolls, Hamlin, etc.) stricter about age bands

### Private K-8 (Town School, Hamlin, Live Oak, Burke, etc.)
- Hardest bar for off-cohort placement
- Big applicant pools = admissions officers err on the side of caution
- Possible for a standout kid but rarely granted

## Redshirting (Holding Back) — Common in SF

**What it is:** Delaying kindergarten by a year so the child is 6 (instead of 5) at K entry.

**Who does it:** Families with **summer birthdays** (June/July/August) whose kids would otherwise be the youngest in class.

**Why it's popular in SF:**
- Bigger, more mature kid has social/academic edge (especially early elementary)
- Better sports trajectory (size matters more than skill through middle school)
- More leadership opportunities
- Backed by research (Gladwell's *Outliers* popularized the "relative age effect")

**Reality:** In competitive SF private K cohorts, **20-40% of the class may be redshirted** — meaning the on-cohort 5-year-old is competing against 6-year-olds socially and physically.

---

## Pushing Up (Early Entrance) — Rare in SF

**What it is:** Entering a grade cohort a year earlier than the Sept 1 cutoff would place you.

**Who considers it:**
- Sept-early-Oct birthdays who miss the cutoff by days/weeks
- Families with a clearly advanced kid (verbally, socially, motor skills)
- Sibling-alignment reasons (want two kids fewer years apart in school)

**Why it's rare:**
- In SF's redshirting-heavy environment, a pushed-up kid faces 14-17 month age gaps with redshirted peers
- Schools are cautious — they're placing the kid for 9+ years, not one
- Can't easily reverse later without a grade-repeat

**What it requires:**
- **SFUSD**: formal cognitive/developmental assessment. Bar is high.
- **Private preschool**: director's observation + family discussion. Usually doable.
- **Private K-8**: full admissions process + above-grade assessment. Hardest.

---

## Decision Framework

### If your kid has a **Sept 2 - Dec birthday**
- **Don't push up** unless the child is visibly advanced across multiple domains AND you have a school willing to support it.
- Being "oldest in class" is a real advantage in SF's competitive cohorts.
- Default to on-cohort placement.

### If your kid has a **Jan - May birthday**
- On-cohort is the standard path.
- Usually no pressure either way.

### If your kid has a **June - Aug birthday**
- **Redshirting is worth considering** if your kid is on the smaller/shyer side, if you have time and childcare flexibility, or if you want maximum runway.
- Many SF families do this by default for summer birthdays, especially for boys.

---

## Key Questions to Ask Schools

When touring preschools, ask the director:

1. **"When's your cutoff and how flexible is it?"**
   - Reveals whether off-cohort placement is even possible.

2. **"How do you handle a [month]-birthday kid — would you place them with their age group or consider moving them?"**
   - Director's answer tells you their philosophy.

3. **"What percentage of your class redshirted / pushed up last year?"**
   - If redshirting is common (40%+), that's a signal about the competitive landscape.

4. **"How do you assess readiness if we're considering early entrance?"**
   - Look for: observation + developmental markers, not just a test.

5. **"What does your school feed into for kindergarten — and what's the age spread in those K classes?"**
   - Important for thinking 2-3 years ahead.

---

## The Boy/Girl Asymmetry

This is often unspoken but widely practiced in SF:

- **Boys** are redshirted more frequently (developmental lag is well-documented — especially fine motor, emotional regulation, sitting still).
- **Girls** are redshirted less frequently.
- Pushing a **girl** up is less risky than pushing a **boy** up of the same age.
- None of this is destiny; lots of summer-birthday boys thrive on-cohort. But the statistical pattern is real.

---

## TL;DR

- **Cutoff is Sept 1.** A kid born Sept 2 misses by one day.
- **SFUSD K**: 5 by Sept 1. Hard rule.
- **Private preschools**: flexible; just ask.
- **Private K-8**: hardest to move off-cohort; usually apply on-grade.
- **Redshirting summer birthdays is common in SF** — so a pushed-up Sept-born kid might face peers 14-17 months older.
- **On-cohort is the safe default.** Push up only if you have strong readiness signals AND an enthusiastic school.
